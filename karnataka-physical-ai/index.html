    How the model works

    Seats scale with area (30 / 60 / 80 / 160). A cohort runs four 2-hour slots a day on the same seats; four cohorts a year. One certified faculty member per 15 seats plus a lab lead. Projects assume teams of four. Capex ranges are per-zone kit estimates scaled by area, calibrated so a 500 sq ft, three-zone lab lands at ₹8–16 lakh — the pilot planning assumption.     A robot is only as useful as the engineer who can build it.

    Drag to rotate

    Day 01First contact

    Capability

    1. Day 01

      First contact

      Safety induction, PPE, a team of four and a robot that is switched off. The first lesson is that hardware does not forgive.

      Can: name every subsystem on the bench

    2. Week 02

      Perception

      LiDAR scans, IMU drift, camera calibration. The world stops being a dataset and becomes noisy, partial and live.

      Can: read and filter a live sensor stream

    3. Week 04

      ROS 2 + simulation

      Nodes, topics, transforms. The same code runs in simulation and on the T-Bot fleet in the maze arena.

      Can: move code from sim to robot

    4. Week 06

      Navigation

      SLAM, localisation against AprilTags, path planning around people and pallets.

      Can: map a room and navigate it autonomously

    5. Week 08

      Control

      PID and LQR loops at 1 kHz on a quadruped. Tuning gains until the robot stops falling over.

      Can: stabilise a physical system

    6. Week 10

      Project build

      An industry-defined brief: vision-guided pick-and-place on a 6-DOF arm. Design, print, wire, test.

      Can: build to a specification

    7. Week 12

      Integration

      Perception, planning and control in one machine — an AMR that finds, lifts and delivers a payload.

      Can: integrate a full robotic system

    8. Final

      Demonstration

      A practical exam on working hardware, assessed independently. Not a quiz. A robot that works, or doesn’t.

      Proven: practical competence on hardware

    The lab doesn’t run itself.

    Faculty capability is infrastructure. Every lab is handed over with certified people who can run it after we leave.

    1. TrainFaculty complete the same hardware journey as students, before the first cohort.
    2. CertifyPractical certification on the lab’s own equipment.
    3. MentorCo-teach the first cohort alongside industry mentors.
    4. OperateRun the timetable, maintenance and safety SOPs.
    5. SustainTrain the next faculty. The capability stays with the college.

    What could go wrong?

    A serious proposal names its failure modes before anyone else does.

    RiskEarly signalMitigation
    1. Labs sit idle.Utilisation below 50% of scheduled hours.

      Timetable integration agreed before installation. Modules credit-mapped to KSDA / NSQF so lab hours count. Utilisation published weekly.

    2. Faculty uptake is low.Fewer than two certified faculty per lab by day 45.

      Train-the-trainer before deployment. Mentors co-teach the first cohort. Certification recognised in faculty development records.

    3. Training does not translate to jobs.Employers do not recognise the certificate.

      Industry-defined capstones. Independent practical exam on hardware. Placement traced at six and twelve months.

    4. Procurement takes too long.Pilot slips past day 35 without installed labs.

      Use an existing route — MoU, KSDC empanelment or tender. Standard equipment list. Phased delivery, zone by zone.

    5. The programme becomes too large, too early.Scale-up proposed before day-100 evidence.

      Hard decision gate. Expansion stage-gated on published outcomes, never on enthusiasm.

    6. Policy ownership is fragmented.Decisions wait on multiple departments.

      One nodal officer. A small steering group across Higher Education, Skills and IT/BT. One shared outcome framework.
